# Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

> To govern the use of Generative Artificial Intelligence Large Language Models (GenAI).

**Version:** 3.00  
**Policy date:** 12 February 2025  
**Classification:** OFFICIAL

## Summary

“GenAI” is a type of artificial intelligence that creates new content like text, images, and videos. It learns from existing data and uses this knowledge to generate new content based on user prompts.

This policy applies to all CEC users with access to authorised GenAI, through council-owned devices or BYOD (bring your own device) in pursuit of council activities.

The only GenAI applications currently authorised are Microsoft Copilot Chat and Microsoft Copilot for Microsoft 365.

Further explanations and guides for use can be found in the policy detail.

## 1. Purpose

### 1.1

The purpose of this policy document is to provide a framework for the use of market and approved Generative Artificial Intelligence Large Language Models (GenAI) by council officers, members, contractors, developers, consultants or other third parties.

### 1.2

The term “Users” applies to users of authorised GenAI tools referenced in [Section 8](#8-approvedbanned-ai-tools), or tools developed through the Digital Workstream. Authorisation of the use of AI is a key principle of this policy and must be adhered to. Users are officers, members, contractors, developers or consultants.

### 1.3

The use of any other personal GenAI application is unauthorised for CEC business and shall not be used. Use of unauthorised GenAI could result in data loss, incorrect information and inappropriate decisions.

### 1.4

This policy is designed to ensure that the use of GenAI is ethical, complies with all applicable laws, regulations and council policies, and sits alongside the council’s existing information and security policies.

### 1.5

The pace of development and application of GenAI is such that this policy will be regularly reviewed and updated every 12 months and approved through the Strategic Information Governance Group (SIGG) and the Digital Workstream. As new challenges in AI arise, these will be addressed and reflected in this policy.

A balanced approach will be adopted, taking into consideration ethics and risks, benefits, and the possibilities of AI. Feedback, suggestions and experiences to improve the policy and ensure that it continues to meet the needs of the council will be addressed by SIGG and the Digital Workstream.

## 2. Use of GenAI

### 2.1

This policy applies to all CEC users with access to authorised GenAI, whether using the tools or developing solutions through council-owned devices or BYOD in pursuit of council activities.

### 2.2

Use of GenAI must be considered and compliant with processes that promote transparency, security and fairness, and which avoid bias to prevent discrimination and promote equal treatment. Inclusive of those principles, the overriding objective of AI is to contribute positively to the council’s goals and values.

### 2.3

Users may use authorised GenAI for work-related purposes only and subject to adherence to the principles and procedures set out in this policy. The term “Users” means any authorised user.

### 2.4

Particular attention should be given to governance, vendor practices, copyright, accuracy, confidentiality, disclosure, ethical use and integration with other tools. Each aspect is considered within this section.

### 2.5 Permitted purposes

GenAI can be used for the following purposes:

1. To analyse vast amounts of data to improve decision-making and optimise processes.
2. To search public documents, such as wider internet resources, for research, national and central government statistics, policies and strategies.

### 2.6 Vendors and the supply chain

Any use of GenAI technology in pursuit of council activities should be made with full authorisation and acknowledgement of the policies, practices, terms and conditions of developers and third-party vendors. Contract managers should note that written acknowledgement must be incorporated within all new procurement contracts.

### 2.7 Copyright

Users must adhere to copyright laws when using GenAI. It is prohibited to use GenAI to generate content that infringes upon the intellectual property rights of others, including copyrighted material. If reference is made to a website, article or publication, it is important to cite the author’s name, publication title, date and/or website URL.

> **Note:** If a user is unsure whether a particular use of GenAI constitutes copyright infringement, they should contact the Information Governance Collaboration Group before using GenAI.

### 2.8 Accuracy

Line managers are responsible for ensuring all information generated by GenAI is reviewed and edited as necessary to ensure accuracy before use. Users are responsible for fact-checking output and are accountable for ensuring the accuracy of GenAI-generated output before use and/or release. Senior managers are accountable for published reports containing AI-generated material.

> **Note:** If a user has any doubt about the accuracy of information generated by GenAI, they should not use it.

### 2.9 Confidentiality

Confidential and personal information must not be entered into a GenAI tool, as information may enter the public domain. Users must follow all applicable data privacy laws and organisational policies when using GenAI.

> **Note:** If a user has any doubt about the confidentiality of information, they should not use GenAI.

### 2.10 Ethical use

GenAI must be used ethically and in compliance with all applicable legislation, regulations and council policies. Users must not use GenAI to generate content that is discriminatory, offensive or inappropriate.

> **Note:** If there are doubts about the appropriateness of using GenAI in a particular situation, users should consult their line manager, supervisor or the Information Governance Collaboration Group.

### 2.11 Disclosure

Content produced via GenAI, or any other AI model, must be identified and disclosed as containing GenAI-generated information.

Example footnote:

> Note: This document may contain information that has been generated by Artificial Intelligence (AI). The Council takes responsibility for this content.

### 2.12 Integration with other tools and systems

API and plugin tools enable access to GenAI and extended functionality for other services to improve automation and productivity outputs.

Assessments are made through the Information Assurance Questionnaire for all technical and security aspects as part of the procurement process. Developers of GenAI will also be instructed to follow OpenAI’s safety best practices:

- Adversarial testing
- Human in the loop (HITL)
- Prompt engineering
- “Know your customer” (KYC)
- Constrain user input and limit output tokens
- Allow users to report issues
- Understand and communicate limitations
- End-user IDs
- Risk assessment
- Authorisation

### 2.13 Testing of APIs and plugins

API and plugin tools must be rigorously tested for:

- **Moderation:** Ensure the model properly handles hateful, discriminatory or threatening inputs.
- **Factual responses:** Provide a ground truth for the API and review responses accordingly.

## 3. Risks

### 3.1 General risks

Artificial Intelligence creates opportunities for innovation, growth and prosperity but also creates a range of new risks. These include damage to physical and mental health, bias and discrimination, and infringements on privacy and individual rights. These risks must be proportionately addressed to benefit from the opportunities AI provides.

Senior managers in the council have a responsibility to regulate risks internally through departmental service plans, and the council is regulated by legislation.

### 3.2 Data protection impact assessment

When developing GenAI or adopting other AI tools, it is essential to undertake a full Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before proceeding.

Reference in source: “Complete a Data Protection or Privacy Impact Assessment” on the Cheshire East Council website.

### 3.4 Legal compliance

Data entered into non-approved GenAI may enter the public domain. This can release non-public information, breach regulatory requirements, customer or vendor contracts, or compromise intellectual property. Any release of private or personal information without the authorisation of the council will result in a breach of relevant data protection laws.

Any new use of technology will require a separate or amended DPIA. Use of GenAI to compile content may also infringe regulations protecting intellectual property rights.

> **Note:** Users should ensure their use of any GenAI complies with all applicable laws, regulations and council policies. Any breach or potential breach of the council’s data protection obligations must be reported to the Data Protection Officer.

### 3.5 Bias and discrimination

GenAI may use and generate biased, discriminatory or offensive content.

> **Note:** Users should use GenAI responsibly and ethically, in compliance with council policies and applicable laws and regulations. If bias or discriminatory matters or references are evident in AI-generated content, they must be corrected or removed before use or distribution.

### 3.6 Security

GenAI may store sensitive data and information that could be at risk of being breached or hacked. Through procurement and technical assessments, the council must assess the protections and security certification of any GenAI not approved within this policy before use.

> **Note:** If a user has any doubt about the security of information input into GenAI, they should not use GenAI.

### 3.7 Data sovereignty and protection

While a GenAI platform may be hosted internationally, under data sovereignty rules information created or collected in the originating country will remain under the jurisdiction of that country’s laws. The reverse also applies. If information is sourced from GenAI hosted overseas, the laws of the source country regarding its use and access may apply.

> **Note:** GenAI service providers must be assessed for data sovereignty practices before use by CEC.

## 4. Benefits and challenges

The use of AI has the potential to reduce costs, increase productivity and creativity, and offer technological compatibility with current systems.

### 4.1 Benefits

AI can offer numerous benefits, including increased efficiency and the automation of repetitive tasks.

#### 4.1.1 Productivity

AI can significantly enhance internal staff productivity by automating tasks and providing summaries, leading to time savings and increased efficiency.

#### 4.1.2 Data analysis and decision-making

AI aids data analysis, improving decision-making processes by providing insights and recommendations.

#### 4.1.3 Technology-enabled care

AI-powered sensors and technology can enhance care for vulnerable people, detecting emergencies and ensuring timely interventions.

#### 4.1.4 Cost management

AI-driven systems, such as temperature control, can help manage costs and optimise building environments, providing cost-effective solutions.

#### 4.1.5 Positive community impact

Emphasising the community impact of AI initiatives can help garner support and alleviate concerns among staff and residents.

#### 4.1.6 Outcome-oriented approach

Focusing on positive outcomes and aligning AI initiatives with the fundamental purpose of local authorities can create a more favourable narrative.

### 4.2 Challenges

#### 4.2.1 Transparency

Emphasise the importance of understanding AI systems’ decision-making processes and addressing bias through plain and simple language.

#### 4.2.2 AI updates

Be aware of AI updates and their potential impact, especially in internal settings. Assess the need for updates and their compatibility with existing AI models.

#### 4.2.3 Software bill of materials

It is important to manage and understand the technical details and components of AI so that software risks and benefits can be assessed effectively.

#### 4.2.4 Ethical considerations, bias and fairness

Using AI can present unfair or prejudiced outcomes in decision-making processes. Carefully consider ethical implications and ensure use cases are well thought out and thoroughly managed.

#### 4.2.5 Monitoring for bias

Regularly monitor AI for bias and make adjustments to mitigate the risk of unintentional bias.

#### 4.2.6 Data quality

While sophisticated, AI may still produce results containing errors due to misunderstanding, lack of understanding or misinterpretation. Ensure all AI-produced material and decisions are fact-checked and monitored.

#### 4.2.7 Reliability

Conduct testing across AI systems to assess how AI performs in practical scenarios before deployment.

#### 4.2.8 Security

Keep the AI system up to date with software updates, improvements and changes as necessary.

## 5. Compliance and governance

### 5.1 Non-adherence

Any non-adherence to this policy should be reported to the Head of Service and escalated through the Digital Workstream and/or Information Governance Collaboration Group. Failure to comply may result in disciplinary action in accordance with the council’s Human Resources policies and procedures.

If this policy is breached, the relevant Human Resources Business Partner will be contacted and will handle matters relating to the breach.

### 5.2 Requests to use unapproved GenAI

Before accessing GenAI technology not approved by this policy, users must notify the council’s Digital Workstream and/or their ICT Account Manager of:

- Their requirements and intention to use the technology
- The reason for use
- The information expected to be input
- The generated output
- The intended distribution of content

#### 5.2.1 New technology or process

Requests involving a new technology or process should be presented to the council’s Digital Workstream and/or the ICT Account Manager as a business case.

#### 5.2.2 Routine questions

Routine questions involving GenAI should be emailed to the council’s Information Governance Collaboration Group.

### 5.3 Data breaches

If a data breach occurs, the individual must report it using the incident report form on the incident-reporting Centranet page. Responsibility for raising awareness of data breaches lies with the council’s Information Governance Group.

### 5.4 Information Asset Register

If information is regularly processed using GenAI, the individual is responsible for updating the council’s Information Asset Register by contacting the Records Management team with the relevant information.

## 6. Education and training

### 6.1

Although formal training has not been deemed necessary for this policy, senior managers and line managers must ensure all CEC staff are made aware of its existence and contents. All GenAI users must be aware of their obligations under this policy.

Further information may be shared through informative emails, Centranet announcements, content within the ICT Lighthouse hub, Bright Sparks, team meetings and newsletters. Specific training on AI interactions and prompts will be contained within training guides for solutions where AI is in use.

## 7. Responsibilities

### 7.1 All users

All users are responsible for abiding by the rules and conditions set out in this policy. Users should:

1. Fact-check all GenAI outputs and ensure information is correct and accurate.
2. Use GenAI data responsibly and consider the ethical matters stated in this policy.
3. Not use personal or sensitive council-owned data on unauthorised GenAI platforms.

### 7.2 Overall responsibility

Overall responsibility for this policy and GenAI lies with the Senior Responsible Officer for the Director of Digital.

### 7.3 Concerns and suggestions

Individuals with concerns or suggestions relating to GenAI should report them to the Bright Sparks group. That group will report them to the council’s Digital Workstream and/or Information Governance Collaboration Group, which will review and escalate them if required.

## 8. Approved/banned AI tools

### 8.1 Approved applications

The only GenAI applications currently authorised are:

- Microsoft Copilot Chat
- Microsoft Copilot for Microsoft 365, with access limited and controlled through a governance process

The policy states that user and business data are protected and will not leak outside the council, chat data is not saved, and chat data is not used to train other AI models.

### 8.2 Unauthorised applications

The use of any other AI application or system is not approved and is therefore unauthorised. Updates to this policy will include approved applications and will be published periodically. Users are responsible for checking the current version of the policy.

## 9. Acknowledgement

### 9.1

By using GenAI, users acknowledge that they have read and understood this policy, including the risks associated with GenAI, and agree to be bound by it.

## 10. Review

### 10.1

This policy will be reviewed periodically and updated as necessary to ensure continued compliance with all applicable legislation, regulations, organisational policies, ethical considerations, and the benefits and challenges of AI.
